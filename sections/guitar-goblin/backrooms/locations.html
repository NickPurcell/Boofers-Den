Location scouting

What the North Bay actually has, which kinds of building work, and who to ring. Desk research only so far — nothing here has been walked, and the last section is the list of calls that turns this into real candidates.

Read this first. These are market figures and building types, not verified vacancies. Listing sites go stale fast and the good short-term deals are rarely posted at all. Treat this as the brief you hand a broker, not a shortlist.

The real constraint is size, not price. Median office listing in Fairfield is about 2,050 sf and the average around 5,150 sf. You want ~6,000 sf contiguous, on one floor, ideally the whole building. That is the minority of listings. Search on floor plate first and rent second — the rent is negotiable in a 14.2% vacancy market, the geometry is not.

Building types, ranked

1. Class B office floor — best fit

A whole floor or whole small building, still fitted out from its last tenant. Suspended ceiling grid with the sprinkler heads already through it, exit signs, alarm, HVAC, restrooms, carpet. Cubicle-era partitions are most of your set already.

Look for: vacated back-office, insurance, mortgage or medical-admin space. Avoid: anything stripped to shell, and anything in a building with other tenants working during your hours.

2. Vacant mall anchor or inline retail — strong on terms

The overlooked option, and the one the seasonal-Halloween industry is actually built on. Solano Town Center in Fairfield has a former Sears anchor; a 54,000 sf divisible specialty space has also appeared in Solano County listings.

Why it is attractive: mall landlords do short seasonal deals cheaply because the alternative is dark frontage. You inherit parking, restrooms, sprinklers and genuine foot traffic, and retail is already a step closer to assembly than office is.

The catch: ceiling height. Department-store floors are often open to structure, which puts you straight back into the false-ceiling-under-sprinklers problem. Inline mall units and the office portions of a former anchor usually do have grid. Check the ceiling before anything else.

3. Warehouse or flex — documented failure case

Cheapest per square foot and the wrong building. Nothing overhead, so you build the grid, the lighting, the sprinkler drops, the HVAC and the restrooms. See the plan for why the sums do not work below about a year's run.

What to ask on every call

  • Is it sprinklered, and does the ceiling grid have heads through it? If no to either, stop there.
  • Contiguous square footage on one floor? Not the building total.
  • Who else is in the building, and what hours? Evening attraction next to a working office is a fight you will lose.
  • Will the landlord do three months? Most will say no first. Vacancy is 14.2% and rising, so ask twice.
  • Free rent during buildout? This is the concession being given right now and it is worth more than a rate cut, because buildout is exactly when you are paying for a building you cannot open.
  • Will they consent to a change of occupancy to assembly? Ask early. Some landlords simply will not, and it is better to hear it on the phone.
  • Parking count. Assembly needs more stalls per square foot than office.

The call list

In this order. The first two cost nothing and change everything downstream.

1Vallejo Fire PreventionSpecial amusement area in a B-occupancy office. What do you need, what will you not allow, how long is plan review?

3Solano Town Center leasingFormer Sears anchor and any dark inline units. Seasonal terms. Ceiling type.
4Two local commercial brokersHand them the brief: 5,000–8,000 sf contiguous, sprinklered, whole floor, 3 months, Sept–Nov.
5Vallejo & Fairfield economic developmentThey know the dark buildings that never get listed, and they like tenants who bring footfall downtown.
Do not skip #5. City economic development offices keep informal lists of vacant space and are motivated to fill it. A seasonal attraction that brings people into a quiet downtown is exactly the thing they want to be seen enabling, and they can be unreasonably helpful about permits.

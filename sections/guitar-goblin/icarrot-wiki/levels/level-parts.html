    How levels are built

    A level is a scene the owner edits in Godot's 2D editor, built from the parts below. Collision, the enemies' route map and the art all come from these parts, so moving a ledge or resizing a block needs no code. Every part draws its own art in code, in the game's pixel style, so it can be any size. The art is painted once, when the level loads. On desktop the big ground blocks paint on a background thread, so for the first few frames of a level they can show up blank.

    Parts go in the level scene's folders:

    • Geometry/: Solid, Plank, Shroom, Door and Pedestal (everything you collide with).
    • Cameras/: CameraZone.
    • Spawns/: SpawnPoint.
    • Foreground/: ForegroundPiece.
    • Anywhere: Torch and Prop, which are decoration only.

    Ground and platforms

    Solid

    A block nothing passes through: ground, walls, ceilings and raised steps. Its top is walkable, and enemies route over it. Set its size. Its look is NONE (invisible, for walls at the edge of the level or ground already painted into the backdrop), the arena's grassy dirt, or one of the underground biomes' ground. Each block is painted across its whole area, so a big floor doesn't repeat.

    Plank

    A one-way ledge: jump up through it from below, land on top, and press S to drop through. Set its width. Its look is the arena's wooden plank or a biome's branch ledge. Enemies jump up to planks, lunge across gaps and drop off them.

    Shroom

    A giant mushroom whose cap is a one-way platform, like a Plank, with a stem reaching down. Set the cap's width, how far the stem reaches, and which biome it belongs to. Its glowing spots and gills bloom.

    Encounter parts

    These are used by encounters such as the Burrow Encounter.

    Door

    A gate of lashed roots that opens and shuts. While it's shut it's a Solid; open, you walk through. A tall box is a door, whose bars slide up into the ceiling. A wide box is a floor gate, whose halves slide apart. closed sets how it starts: boss room doors start open, and the hub's floor gate starts shut. The encounter opens and shuts them for everyone at once.

    Pedestal

    A one-way ledge with a strip of runes in its key's colour, marking where that key's orb goes. The runes glow faintly until the orb is placed, then blaze, and the orb hovers above the pedestal. Set its width, look and key.

    Torch

    A wall torch burning in a key's colour, so players can tell which room holds which orb. It flickers and blooms, and has no collision. Set its key.

    The three key colours come from one shared palette, so the torches, pedestals and orbs always match.

    Camera and spawns

    CameraZone

    A box that gives a fixed camera shot. While your carrot is inside it, the camera stops following you and frames the whole box, zooming to fit it. Everywhere else the camera follows you. Each machine frames its own carrot, so co-op players can be in different shots. Arenas and boss rooms use one.

    SpawnPoint

    A marker, only visible in the editor:

    • PLAYER: where a player drops in. Set its slot (P1, P2 or P3).
    • ENEMY: where enemies walk in. In wave levels, the outermost one on each side is used.
    • PERCH: a spot on a plank where perching enemies like the asparagus land.

    Decoration

    ForegroundPiece

    A blurred silhouette in front of everything, such as roots hanging from a ceiling or the edge of a wall, that slides a little faster than the world as the camera moves so it reads as close to the camera. It sits exactly where it's placed in the editor, and in game when the camera is centred on it. Elsewhere it drifts depth times the camera's distance from it, sideways by default. Pieces hung from a ceiling or set against a wall keep depth.y at 0, so they stay attached as the camera moves up and down. Each machine's pieces follow its own camera, so they look right for every co-op player.

    Prop

    A decoration with no collision: a giant carrot, mushrooms on a wall, a fence. It can have a glow layer that blooms, and it can sway in the breeze around the point it grows from.

    Tunable parameters

    These are read straight from the game's files, so they are always the current values. Open the file in the Godot Inspector to change them.

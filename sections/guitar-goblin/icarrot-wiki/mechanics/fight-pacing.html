    The idea

    A boss fight shouldn't be one long, even wall of enemies. Each boss room's fight is a short list of beats: an opening that shows what the fight is about, a breather, a spike, a valley where you feel powerful, and a climax as the boss dies. Every beat brings in a squad that was picked by hand to suit that boss, at a spawn group placed by hand in the room. Nothing is random or generated, so the fight plays the way it was designed.

    It's built in three layers, all edited in Godot:

    Layer What it answers Where it lives
    Spawn groups Where enemies come in Spawn markers in the room, each with a group name
    Squads What comes in, and how Small files in styles/squads/
    Beats When it comes in Beat nodes under the boss room, in fight order

    Spawn groups

    A boss room's spawn markers (RoomSpawn, role ADD) are placed in the 2D editor, each with a group name such as "floor", "ledges", "perches" or "high". A beat names the group its squad comes in at. Squad members are shared out across the group's markers in turn, and several at one marker spread out to either side. A marker set to emerge makes enemies burst up out of the ground there.

    Squads

    A squad is a reusable file listing which enemy kinds come in and how many of each. Each line can name a spawn group of its own, so snipers go to the perches while the rest come in on the floor. stagger is the time between one arrival and the next (0 means all at once), and arrivals are mixed (zombie, pumpkin, zombie, pumpkin) rather than one kind at a time.

    Threat

    Every enemy kind has a threat cost in styles/threat_costs.tres. It's roughly the damage the enemy threatens per second, times how long it lives under fire, times how hard it is to ignore. A zombie is the unit. A squad's threat is the sum over its members, and it's how a fight's difficulty curve is designed and checked. Bosses aren't counted: their own health bar tells that story.

    Beats

    Beats are nodes under their boss room. Tree order is fight order: drag a beat up or down in the scene tree to reorder the fight, and click one to edit it in the Inspector. Beats always fire one at a time, in order.

    Triggers

    Trigger Fires when
    FIGHT_START the room's doors shut
    BOSS_HEALTH this much of the boss's health has been taken (boss_health_removed, 0 to 1)
    CLEARED the previous beat's squad is down to remaining or fewer
    TIMER seconds after the previous beat began
    EVENT an encounter event of that name happens (a boss's own moment, say)

    The Inspector only shows the setting the chosen trigger uses. More triggers can be added to the list later.

    Options

    • Squad and group: who comes in, and at which spawn group. No squad makes the beat a rest.
    • Leftovers: enemies from earlier beats still standing when this one starts either STAY (the default, so a valley is the players mopping up) or are REMOVEd.
    • Boss immune: the boss can't be hurt until this beat's squad is cleared. Its bar shows IMMUNE, and hits show IMMUNE instead of numbers. Off by default.
    • Min time: the least time this beat lasts before the next can start. A burst of damage (a rocket over two thresholds at once) then can't skip or stack beats.
    • Warning: the time between the warning and the squad arriving.
    • Drop: an ammo brick (special or heavy) for every player as the beat starts. The valleys use this.

    The warning

    Before a squad arrives, every machine hears a low horn swell, the screen gives a small shudder, and orange rings with a "!" pulse at each spawn marker the squad will use. A spike reads as an event, and players can see where the next wave is coming from.

    The difficulty curve

    The owner drew the curve for the first boss rooms against the fight's progress, from the boss at full health to the boss dying. The thresholds below are the Red Corn room's, and the other rooms use the same shape:

    Beat Boss health taken (0 to 1) Intensity (of the climax) Feel
    Opening from the start about 30% a real opening, showing what the fight is
    Breather 0.2 about 15% a lone straggler and the opening's leftovers
    Spike 0.4 about 65% the first big spike
    Valley once the spike is cleared (or at 0.7) under 10% a rest, with an ammo drop
    Climax 0.8 100% the hardest it has been

    Building a curve that holds

    Measuring the first version (the perf tool's Fight pacing table) showed what makes a beat feel the way it's drawn:

    • A wave that arrives all at once is cleared at once. Its peak is right, but most of the beat is empty. Spikes and climaxes come in two waves: the beat itself, then a TIMER beat a few seconds later (Spike2, Climax2).
    • Leftovers carry over. A breather that brings a full squad stacks on the opening's stragglers and ends up hotter than the opening. The breathers bring a single Rot Runner.
    • A valley must wait for the spike to clear, or it's just the spike's leftovers. The valley uses CLEARED, with a boss-health fallback so a squad nobody clears (snipers out of reach) can't stall the fight.
    • Long-lived enemies hold a beat up. Snipers on perches and asparagus on ledges stay until someone hunts them, so they go where staying is the point (the corn's snipers come with its climax) rather than in an opening they'd outlast.

    Reading the sketch

    The sketch's axis is read as how much of the boss's health has been taken, because the curve ends on its highest point as the boss dies. If it meant health remaining, the beats reverse.

    The knobs

    • Per boss room (the BossRoom node, Pacing group):
    • intensity scales every squad in that fight.
    • players_scale multiplies squad sizes for 1, 2 and 3 players. More players face more enemies, not tougher ones: extra bodies split the squad's fire, where extra health would only make the fight longer.
    • Per beat: the trigger and its setting, the squad, the group, and the options above.
    • Per squad: its members and stagger.
    • Shared: the threat costs.
    • Warnings (the Encounter node, Beats group): the shake, ring colour and glow, and how far apart arrivals at one marker spread.

    Seeing the curve without playing

    In the 2D editor, each boss room draws a bar per beat above itself: as tall as the beat's threat, labelled with its threat number, name and trigger. readout_players picks which player count the bars show. Change a squad or the room's intensity and the bars update.

    Co-op

    The host runs the beats and owns every enemy. When a beat starts, it tells every machine, so everyone hears and sees the same warning. A player who joins mid-fight is told which beat each room is on and whether its boss is immune. A squad wipe restarts the level, and the fight from its first beat. Squad sizes follow the number of players in the game when each beat starts.

    Testing and tuning

    • --beat=N starts every boss room's fight at beat N, with the boss's health set to where that beat begins, so the climax can be tuned without replaying the opening.
    • Every enemy kind a squad can bring in is prepared while the level loads (see Encounter.kinds()), so no squad stutters as it arrives.
    • python tools/perf/run.py burrow-mid-fight (also burrow-top-fight and burrow-bot-fight) plays a room's whole fight with a bot. Its report's Fight pacing section has a row per beat (trigger, length, health taken, designed and actual threat) and charts of the threat alive against the design.

The reasoning behind the current numbers (zombie = 1): - zombie: slow melee that rarely reaches a moving player; dead in under a second. - pumpkin: dies fast, but sprints at you and its blast must be killed or dodged first. - asparagus: fragile, but perched out of reach chipping away; someone has to go and hunt it. - mushroom: the most damage of the small adds, and it backs off, so it takes chasing. - pepper: lobs that deny ground plus a breath, and it lasts twice as long as a zombie. - potato: a tank whose charge forces a dodge every few seconds; it splits the squad's attention. - corn: a mini-boss with volleys of kernels; a squad on its own.

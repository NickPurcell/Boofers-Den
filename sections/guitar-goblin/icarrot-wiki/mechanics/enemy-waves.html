    Enemy waves

    Enemy waves
    First arrival
    1.5 s
    Replacement after a kill
    2.5 s

    Outside raid encounters, a level keeps a set roster of enemies about. Each arrives in turn from the edges, and each one killed is replaced by another of its kind.

    How it works

    A level with enemy waves on keeps a roster of enemies about, one of each entry in its list (a kind listed twice means two of them). The first one arrives 1.5 s after the level starts and the rest follow 0.8 s apart, alternating between the right and left edges.

    When an enemy dies, another of the same kind arrives 2.5 s later. It comes in from whichever side is away from the players, so it doesn't drop in on top of anyone. The pressure never lets up: the roster is always back to full strength soon after a kill.

    Where enemies come in is set by the level's ENEMY spawn markers (the outermost one on each side). Snipers like Spear-Gus start on PERCH markers instead.

    Levels without waves

    Turning enemy_waves off on a level's root node gives a level with no roster at all. Calm levels like the Garden intro use this. So do Encounters, which bring in their own enemies room by room.

    Co-op

    The host runs all the timers and decides where each enemy arrives; everyone else sees the enemies appear. See Co-op.

    For level makers

    Each level scene has its own roster and timings: select the level's root node and look under Enemy waves in the Inspector. The default roster has two zombies and one of each other regular enemy.

    Tunable parameters

    These are read straight from the game's files, so they are always the current values. Open the file in the Godot Inspector to change them.

    Following your carrot

    Levels are bigger than the screen. Each level sets how many game pixels the screen shows (view_size; normally every game pixel is 2x2 screen pixels at 1080p), and the camera glides after your carrot, catching up at 7 /s. It aims a little above your feet (follow_offset), so you see more of what's above and ahead. It never shows past the level's edges. A level smaller than the view is zoomed in until it fills the screen.

    When you fall faster than 300 px/s, the camera starts leading below you, up to 140 px at full fall speed, so you can see where you're going to land on the way down a shaft.

    Getting hurt, firing and big impacts shake the camera; how hard depends on what happened.

    Camera zones: fixed shots

    Arenas and boss rooms use camera zones. While your carrot is inside a zone, the camera stops following and holds a fixed shot of the whole zone: centred on it, and zoomed so the zone fills the screen. It glides into and out of the shot at the zone's own ease speed.

    The zone goes by the middle of your carrot, not its feet, so hopping along a floor at a zone's bottom edge doesn't flick you in and out. Once the camera has switched to a zone's shot, it keeps it until you're 12 px outside the zone. That way hovering on the edge doesn't make it switch back and forth.

    Co-op

    Every machine frames its own carrot, so players in a squad can be in different places, and even in different zones' shots, at once.

    For level makers

    Add a CameraZone node to a level scene, place its top-left corner, and set its size in the Inspector; it shows as a yellow box in the editor. Draw boss rooms as zones so the whole fight is on screen. A zone's size sets the zoom, so one the same size as the level's view_size shows it at the normal scale.

    History

    • A camera zone drawn down exactly to the floor used to flick between following and the fixed shot on every hop. The zone now goes by the carrot's middle, and the exit margin was added.

    Tunable parameters

    These are read straight from the game's files, so they are always the current values. Open the file in the Godot Inspector to change them.

    Look

    The rotten vegetables' lair. You're inside a compost heap, so every layer of dirt is a cross-section of things people threw away: old boots with flapping soles, apple cores, banana peels, bottle caps, fish bones, eggshell flecks, a rubber duck, and worms peeking out of their tunnels.

    Earthy browns carry it. A sickly-fun lime, the same colour as the enemies' goo, and mustard pick out the details, so the place reads as their home. The toadstools are mouldy teal and ochre with cream spots that glow. It's gross-cute rather than gross: everything is a gag, nothing is slimy-real.

    It comes after the Glowshroom Grotto and before the Taproot Heart.

    What it draws

    Ground blocks (Solid). A litter lip of straw on top, with lime moss patches that make the walkable edge read, over dark mulch flecked with leaves and eggshell. Below that run wavy bands of clay, sand and mustard through warm soil that darkens with depth. Buried in it, spaced so they never crowd: boots, apple cores, banana peels, bottle caps, fish bones, eggshells, the rubber duck, worm tunnels and the odd lime goo seep bubbling out under the lip. Junk deep in the block sinks into the dark with the soil.

    Jump-through ledges (Plank). A twisted root rope of braided strands with mossy tufts on top, a pig's-tail curl at the left end and a snapped end showing its rings at the right. Rootlets, a goo drip and sometimes a dangling worm hang underneath.

    Mushroom platforms (Shroom).

    • Cap: a mouldy toadstool, either teal or ochre with teal mould (picked from the node's name), with cream spots, a full-width brim that curls down at the ends and a lens of gills underneath.
    • Glow: the spot cores glow a minty cream; on teal caps the gills glow too.
    • Stem: cream, lit on the left, with a frilly skirt and a gentle bend. Teal mould creeps up from a bulb sitting in a few dirt clods. Short stems get a sleepy face: closed eyes and a little "o" of a snore.

    Backdrop. Three baked layers, seamless in both directions.

    • Far: the near-black wall of the heap with soft dithered strata swelling across it, junk sunk into it as single-tone shapes one shade off the soil, faint roots, and a few hollows where tiny teal toadstools glow. This layer barely moves.
    • Mid: lumpy heap pillars running the full height, with soft strata inside, half-buried junk and roots trailing between them. It drifts a little more, and the far wall shows through the gaps.
    • Near: two thick twisted roots, with an old boot dangling from one by its laces and a worm hanging off the other. It moves the most of the three.

    Foreground. Blurred silhouettes in front of the playfield: a hanging clump with a banana peel draped over a root and an apple core stuck in it, an old boot dangling by its laces, a worm dangling from a rootlet and curling up to look at the carrots, a side wall with a fish skeleton sticking out, a bottom heap with two toadstools, an apple core and a banana peel, and a heap with a boot sticking out toe-up.

    Using it in a level

    • On a Solid set look to COMPOST, on a Plank set look to COMPOST, and on a Shroom set biome to COMPOST. See Level Parts.
    • Baked files are in art/baked/compost_*: compost_bg_far, compost_bg_mid, compost_bg_near, and the foreground pieces compost_fg_hang, _hang_boot, _hang_worm, _side, _bottom and _bottom_boot, each with a _blur version for the game. scenes/compost_test.tscn shows the setup.
    • Hanging foreground pieces only look right where they can hang from something (a ceiling, or the top of the level). In a tall shaft that scrolls up and down they float, so leave them out there.
    • To re-bake, run tools/underground/compost_depths/bake.gd as a headless Godot script, then run a headless import once so the new images are picked up.

    Design notes

    • The carrots must pop. The first art pass had a backdrop as busy and bright as the playfield. Now every backdrop layer stays well below the playfield's soil, and the backdrop junk is single-tone, one shade off the soil, so nothing back there passes for a pickup. Lime glows only on the lip moss, goo seeps and drips, matching the enemies' goo, so their hits still stand out by being brighter.
    • Nothing in the backdrop may look like a platform. The strata are soft and dithered, never a crisp line, and everything in the mid and near layers runs up and down. A goo vein inside the ground was dropped because it read as a second grass line.
    • The foreground blur is dithered. Edges soften in a few see-through steps with an ordered dither, with a faint olive rim light on the lit side so the dark shapes still read over a dark cave.
    • Known weak spots: in the backdrop the duck and the tin can can still read as pickups at a glance; worms that wiggle, dripping goo and twinkling spots would sell the "alive heap".

    Look

    The Warm Burrow is the first room of the dungeon: a rabbit warren dug just under the garden. It should feel like a safe, cosy place to start. The soil uses the same browns as the surface's dirt blocks, so dropping down a hole into it feels continuous rather than like a level change.

    The joke of the room is that the carrots growing in the garden above push their orange taproots down through the ceiling and walls. Worms peek out of their tunnels, pebbles are stuck in the soil, and little amber glow-caps supply all the light. Further back the warren gets darker and turns plum-brown, so the lit edges of the playfield always read first.

    It comes before the Overgrown Vault in the dungeon's progression.

    What it draws

    Ground blocks (Solid). A block of warm soil of any size with a lit, crumbly top lip and a dark outline down the sides and along the underside. The fill is a calm mottle that gets darker the deeper it goes. Scattered through it are pebbles, fine dark rootlets, the odd worm tunnel (sometimes with a worm in it), "root coins" showing the cut end of a carrot root, and orange carrot taproots pushing down from the surface. Every block is painted fresh, so even a very wide floor never repeats.

    Jump-through ledges (Plank). A twisted root branch with a lit top and braided grain. It has a gnarled knob on the left end, a curly tendril on the right, and pale root hairs hanging below the walking band.

    Mushroom platforms (Shroom).

    • Cap: an amber glow-cap. A domed toadstool top with cream spots, a scalloped brim that overhangs and curls down at both ends, and a lens of gills underneath fanning in towards the stem.
    • Glow: the gills' light stripes and the cores of the spots bloom a warm cream-amber. Because the underside glows, a mushroom lights the carrot standing under it.
    • Stem: cream, lit on the left, with a frilly ring under the gills, a gentle bend and a bulb at the base that darkens into the ground.

    Backdrop. Three baked layers, each seamless in both directions so a level can scroll any way.

    • Far: the far wall of the warren. Flat plum-brown earth with faint strata, distant tunnel bores and round-topped burrow doors, and a few warm lit hollows as focal points. This layer barely moves.
    • Mid: earth pillars joined by natural arches, with root curtains hanging from them and a few dim glow-caps on top. It drifts a little more.
    • Near: two thick roots running down through the room, with clods of earth caught on them and muted carrot taproots dangling from the clods. It moves the most of the three, but still slower than the playfield.

    Foreground. Out-of-focus silhouettes that slide past in front of the playfield, a bit faster than it: a hanging clump of soil with roots and a taproot, one long dangling taproot, a root running across with a curtain of rootlets, a hanging bundle with a worm dangling from it, a side wall with roots poking into the room, and a bottom lip of mounds with root loops and little mushroom silhouettes.

    Using it in a level

    • On a Solid, set look to BURROW. On a Plank, set look to BURROW. On a Shroom, set biome to BURROW (the default). See Level Parts.
    • The backdrop and foreground are baked images in art/baked/burrow_*: burrow_bg_far, burrow_bg_mid, burrow_bg_near, and the foreground pieces burrow_fg_hang, _taproot, _curtain, _hang_worm, _side and _bottom, each with a _blur version. Use the blurred ones in game; the sharp ones are for reference. scenes/burrow_test.tscn shows the full setup: each backdrop layer in its own Parallax2D under Backdrop, and the foreground pieces in one Parallax2D above everything.
    • To re-bake after changing the art, run tools/underground/warm_burrow/bake.gd as a headless Godot script (the command is at the top of the file).

    Design notes

    • The carrots must pop. Everything in the backdrop stays well below the playfield's soil in brightness, and the far layer is the darkest and flattest. Only the mushrooms bloom; soil, roots and backdrop never do, so a glow always means something.
    • Nothing in the backdrop may look like a platform. The near roots run mostly up and down, and the arches are curved, so a player never jumps at a ledge that isn't there.
    • The foreground blur is dithered, not smooth. The silhouettes' edges are softened to a few steps of see-through and mixed with an ordered dither, so they look out of focus while staying clean pixel art. The edge facing the light gets a faint warm rim so the shapes don't vanish against the dark. Foreground roots are drawn thick on purpose, because thin ones turn to fuzz when blurred.
    • Known weak spots (from the art notes): the mushroom cap can still look a little bun-shaped from the side; the soil fill is on the busy side; the middle of the far wall is a big flat dark area that could use a focal point, like a lantern-lit side burrow; and the top foreground corners are heavy. Wiggling worms and drifting dust would bring it to life.

    Running

    A and D run left and right. On the ground the carrot speeds up at 1300 px/s² to a top speed of 115 px/s, and when you let go it stops at 1700 px/s², so it starts quickly and stops almost dead without sliding. In mid-air you can still steer, but more gently: speed changes at 800 px/s².

    The carrot always faces where you aim, not where it runs, so you can back away from an enemy while shooting it. Its boots step in the direction it's actually moving either way (see Appearance).

    Jumping

    Space (or W) jumps, launching at 390 px/s. Gravity pulls at 1050 px/s² on the way up and 1.45x as hard on the way down, so a jump floats at the top and drops back snappily. Falls top out at 440 px/s.

    • Short hops: let go of jump early and the upward speed is cut to 0.45x of what it was, so a tap is a small hop and a held press is a full jump.
    • Coyote time: you can still jump for 0.09 s after running off a ledge.
    • Jump buffer: a jump pressed up to 0.12 s before you land fires on landing.

    Landing hard squashes the carrot flat; a big drop also thumps, throws up dust and shakes your screen.

    Double jump

    Press jump again in mid-air for a second jump at 340 px/s, a little weaker than the ground jump. You get 1 before you have to land, and landing gives them back. It kicks a ring of dust and sparks off your boots.

    Planks and dropping

    Wooden planks and mushroom caps are one-way: jump up through them from below and land on top. Press S while standing on one to drop through it. Solid ground and walls can't be passed in either direction.

    Fixed-step physics

    Movement runs in fixed steps of 1/120 s, separate from the frame rate. A jump always reaches the same height and a dash always covers the same distance, whether the game is running at 30 or 240 frames a second, on desktop or in a browser.

    Co-op

    Your own machine moves your carrot, so it responds instantly. Other players see it from streamed position and velocity, smoothed and nudged slightly ahead to hide the delay.

    Design notes

    The numbers are tuned for a snappy, readable platformer: fast starts and stops, a jump with a soft top and a quick fall, and generous coyote time and jump buffering so that jumps you meant to make happen. Enemies share the same physics with their own tuning files (for example the Zombie).

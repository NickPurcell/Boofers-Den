Size and shape

  • Shell 130 × 96 × 72 mm (wide, deep, tall), printed in coral, with the eyes and claws as separate pieces.
  • Legs at the four corners. Hip servo shaft-down in the frame (swings the leg forward and back), then a 32 mm coxa to the knee servo, then a 64 mm tibia to a TPU foot.
  • Standing: knees 15° out, hips 62 mm off the ground, feet 201 mm apart side to side.
  • Step: 36 mm stride, 14–17 mm foot lift. About 20 mm/s in creep and 90 mm/s in trot at 1×.
  • Mass about 420 g: servos 107 g, cells and holder 105 g, boards 30 g, printed parts 150 g, wire and screws 30 g.

Servo angle = 90 + Dir × joint angle. The left and right legs are mirror images, so their servos turn opposite ways for the same move. If a joint goes the wrong way on your build, flip its Dir in the sketch.

Power, and not browning out

  • Servo rail at 6.0 V (MG90S are rated 4.8–6 V; 6 V gives the most torque, never go above). Each servo pulls about 10 mA idle, 150–250 mA moving and close to 1 A stalled. Walking averages 1–2 A with peaks of 3–4 A, so use a 5 A buck. Eight stalled servos would be 8 A.
  • The ESP32 gets its own 5 V buck. If it shares the servo rail, a few servos starting at once sag the rail and the ESP32 resets ("Brownout detector was triggered" in the serial monitor). With its own supply it doesn't see the dips.
  • 1000 µF across V+ and GND at the PCA9685 soaks up the start-up spikes. Mind the polarity.
  • Soft start. The sketch wakes the servos one at a time, 150 ms apart, instead of all eight jumping into position at once.
  • One common ground for everything, or the I²C signals get flaky.
  • Battery. Two 2500 mAh 18650s are about 18 Wh. At around 9 W of walking that's 1.5 hours. Charge them out of the robot unless your holder has protection and balancing.

Gaits and the joint-angle tables

Both gaits are loops of keyframes. A foot on the ground sweeps back 36 mm (that pushes the body forward), then lifts, swings forward and comes down. Creep also sways the body 12 mm forward before each hind step and back before each front step (all the planted feet shift together), so the centre of mass is never near the edge of the three feet holding it up. The angles come from inverse kinematics: the knee sets the foot's height (knee = acos((62 − height) / 64)), then the hip turns the foot to its spot along the stride (hip = asin(forward / reach), where reach = 32 + 64 sin(knee)).

Values are joint angles in degrees: hip + = foot forward, knee = degrees out from straight down. Shaded knee cells are steps, with the foot in the air. Steering scales each side's hip angles (the outside legs take longer steps), and the playing row lights up as the sim runs.

Why the feet scuff a little (the "foot scuff" number in the sim): a 2-servo leg swings its foot on an arc, so planted feet drift a few millimetres closer together and farther apart over a step. That's about 3 mm in trot, and up to 6 mm in creep, where the sway makes the arcs longer. The sim moves the body by the best fit to the planted feet and shows the leftover instead of hiding it. TPU feet take it in their stride.

Build notes

  • Centre the servos before the horns go on. Run the sketch without sending w: it parks every servo at the first creep keyframe. Fit each horn as close as the spline allows, then fix the last few degrees in TRIM.
  • Torque check. In trot two feet carry 420 g, about 2.1 N each. The foot sits 17 mm out from under the knee, so the knee holds 0.035 N·m (0.36 kg·cm): about 5× margin on an MG90S. The hip servo's shaft also carries the leg sideways. That's fine at this weight, but if you add a lot more, put an M3 pin on the hip axis above the servo as a second bearing.
  • Balance. Thanks to the sway, the centre of mass stays at least 8 mm inside the triangle of planted feet in creep (about 4 mm while turning): that's the "balance margin" in the sim. Without the sway it would touch zero at every leg change and the robot would rock. Keep the battery centred and low, as drawn. Trot has only two feet down at a time and stays up by being quick.
  • Print the frame and legs in PLA at 3 walls and 30% infill; the shell can be 2 walls. Print the eyes in black and press-fit them. The claws hang on 1.2 mm TPU flexures, so they jiggle as it walks: no servos.
  • Next steps: a phone remote over Bluetooth, or an IMU so it can feel when it's tipping.

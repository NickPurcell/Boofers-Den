MH370 and the “orb videos”

Two clips from 2014 seem to show three orbs circling an airliner, a flash, and then nothing. A post this week says a former U.S. Navy officer leaked them. Here is the claim, its best case, and the evidence, point by point.

239 people were on MH370: 227 passengers and 12 crew. All are presumed dead.1

The short answer

The clips’ clouds match stock photographs from 2012, and their flash matches a stock explosion from the 1990s.23 The only way round the photographs is that they were planted with false dates, and no evidence has been offered for that.

The plane went on answering a satellite for six hours after the moment the clips would have it vanish.1

Nothing released ties Edward Lin to the clips.4 None of the 57 pages of his released court papers mentions MH370, a satellite or a video.56 One classified addendum is sealed, and nobody outside government has read it.

Where MH370 lies is still unknown.7

1The claim

Told as its advocates tell it.

The orb videos

In 2014 an anonymous YouTube account, “RegicideAnon”, posted two clips.910

The satellite clip, posted on 19 May 2014, ten weeks after MH370 vanished. A grey, top-down view of a twin-engine airliner over clouds and sea. A mouse cursor drags the view, and coordinates sit half cut off at the bottom. Its description says “Received: 12 March 2014” and “Source: Protected”, and calls the plane a “large twin engine maybe Boeing 777?”. It does not mention MH370.9

The thermal clip, posted on 12 June 2014. A rainbow-coloured heat view of the same scene, describing itself as an “alt angle to previously submitted SAT footage”.10

Both show a 777-like jet, then three small orbs circling it, then a flash. After it, the plane and the orbs are gone.910

The satellite clip, as archived in August 2014Posted 19 May 2014. Opens the Wayback Machine.
The thermal clip, as archived in August 2014Posted 12 June 2014. Opens the Wayback Machine.

How it came back. The clips sat almost unseen until August 2023, when a Reddit post on r/UFOs took off and believers split off into their own forum.3 Rolling Stone ties the revival to the July 2023 UAP hearing in Congress.4 In November 2023 Kim Dotcom backed the theory and offered a $100,000 bounty for the original footage.3

What Ashton Forbes says happened. Forbes, the theory’s best-known advocate, says:4

  • the clips are real U.S. military footage;
  • the orbs are U.S. “plasma orbs” with fusion reactors;
  • the U.S. military teleported the airliner as a show of strength aimed at China (“This is the United States versus China”);
  • the stock cloud photos “must have been planted online by the U.S. intelligence agencies”.

Since 2024 he accepts that the MH370 debris is real, but says it entered the water somewhere other than the official search area.8 4Orbs, a site that argues his case, reports that he calls the satellite data “potentially manipulated”.8

Rolling Stone records his own test for being wrong: find the maker of the clips, find the plane, talk to Lin, or get a U.S. government statement.4

This post: Edward Lin

What we could not read

The post’s list of “strange anomalies” is not public. Past its first lines the post sits behind X’s login, and no outside page quotes it.11

Its 92-second video is the two 2014 clips cut together and set to “Take Me Home, Country Roads”. No court document, caption or text card appears in it.11

So below are the Lin arguments as their advocates make them in public, not a confirmed list of the post’s points.

The post, by @bkseatpassenger on 22 November 2026, says that “Edward Lin is suspected of leaking footage of missing Flight MH370”, that “strange anomalies” in his court documents “suggest @AshtonForbes is correct”, and that “Edward openly admitted to briefing a foreign…”.11

Edward Lin is a former U.S. Navy lieutenant commander who pleaded guilty in 2017 to mishandling and communicating classified information.6

The Lin arguments as they circulate, from Forbes (through Rolling Stone) and the 4Orbs site:412

  1. Lin flew with VPU-2, a once-“black” special-projects squadron, and had Pacific Fleet and JTF 519 access.12
  2. His stipulation says the disclosure law applied to him “in April 2012 to May 2014”. May 2014 is when the satellite clip appeared.12
  3. A Navy insider-threat case study says a tip reached the FBI in “early 2014”. Forbes dates the investigation to about 2 April 2014, weeks after MH370.12
  4. The five espionage counts were dropped, yet he got nine years, which Forbes calls the “right punishment”.4
  5. Twelve classification authorities reviewed what he disclosed. By 4Orbs’s reading of the plea agreement, four of them produced stipulations, including JSOC and AFRICOM, which have nothing to do with patrol flights.12
  6. The details of what he disclosed are in a SECRET addendum that has not been released. The Navy withheld the NCIS file in full, and the FBI would neither confirm nor deny having records.12
  7. 4Orbs reports a caller’s unrecorded account: the caller says Lin’s civilian lawyer spoke of satellite video evidence “the feds brought into the case”. 4Orbs describes it as both a call and a voicemail.12
  8. Forbes, quoted by Rolling Stone: “It has to be him because everything lines up: right time, right place, right access, and right punishment… Tell me anything else it could be that he leaked.”4

Hard to answer, honestly

  • Lin’s SECRET addendum is unreleased. Nobody outside government can prove what it does not say. What can be said: nothing that has been released links him to the clips, and the burden is on the claim.
  • The pre-2014 date of the cloud photos rests on the textures site’s listing and file data, its owner’s word and the photographer’s RAW files. There is no independent archive capture from before 2014.3

  • The debris proves the plane hit the ocean, but on its own it does not pin down where.1

3The evidence

Three findings carry the debunking. Then each point of the case, and what answers it.

These three are evidence in their own right, not answers to one point.

Strong (decisive if you accept the photographer’s original files)

The clouds are stock photographs from 2012

On 7 December 2023 a Reddit user, a VFX artist, posted that the satellite clip’s background matches several photos in the “Aerials0028” set on Textures.com, mirrored left to right.2

The fact-checkers at Lead Stories reproduced the overlay themselves using two of the set’s photos.2

The photographer, concept artist Jonas De Ro, supplied his original RAW files, and the site’s owner checked their dates and vouched for him.3 De Ro says the photos were taken from a plane over Japan in 2012.16

Where it is weaker than people say. No archive capture of the set predates 2016.3 For the match to fail, someone would have had to plant photos on a commercial site with false 2012 dates and fake the photographer’s RAW archive too. Forbes asserts exactly that, with no evidence offered.4

Lead Stories: the overlay, reproducedTheir composites of the clip’s frames on the stock photos.
The Aerials0028 set on Textures.comThe stock photographs themselves.

Strong

The flash is a 1990s stock explosion

The thermal clip’s flash matches the “SHOCKWV” shockwave from “Pyromania! Playing With Fire”, a stock-effects collection first released in 1993–94, recoloured and distorted in the clip.3417

The same element appears in the 1995 game Killing Time, in a 2007 YouTube upload, as Metabunk reports.1819 So the element has a public record from long before 2014, and the “planted asset” answer does not work here.

Where it is weaker than people say. Advocates say the frames match only in part, and that the clip uses 4 frames of a 36-frame element.14 A hoaxer would trim and distort a stock element, but we have not done the frame comparison ourselves.

The Pyromania collectionThe stock effects, on the Internet Archive.
Metabunk’s side-by-sidesThe flash against the stock shockwave, and more.

Strong (decisive if the Inmarsat logs are genuine)

The plane kept answering a satellite for six more hours

Malaysian military radar last saw MH370 at 02:22 Malaysia time. The plane’s satellite terminal then logged on at 02:25 and answered hourly handshakes at 03:41, 04:41, 05:41, 06:41 and 08:10. It sent a last log-on at 08:19 and did not answer at 09:15.1

So the plane was still powered, and the changing frequency values show it was still moving at speed, hours after the moment the clips would have it vanish. The timing values place it on rings (“arcs”) centred under the satellite. With the frequency data, investigators used them to put the end of the flight in the southern Indian Ocean.120

The advocates’ answer. They say the frequency data were “potentially manipulated”.8 That needs Inmarsat’s ground-station logs, and the independent teams who analysed them, to be wrong or complicit.

The coordinates

Strong⌄

Verdict: Numbers that follow the drag tell you how the overlay was made, not that the place is real. The plane was still flying hours after it would have to vanish there.

The case for

The on-screen readout runs from 8.834°N 93.195°E to 8.823°N 93.222°E, near the Nicobar Islands. It changes only while the view is dragged, consistently with north up and east right, across all seven drags.13

The position is north-west of MH370’s last military radar fix, which Forbes calls “the smoking gun”.13

The evidence against

Suggestive Moving coordinates and a shaky “operator” are things an artist can animate

A readout that updates while a layer is dragged is ordinary compositing.

Metabunk notes that the on-screen display at one point goes behind the plane, which would be a layering error.18 Mick West reports that the contrails wobble against the plane when the footage is stabilised.3

Suggestive Daylight at midnight

The satellite clip shows bright, sunlit clouds with shaded sides.9

On 8 March 2014 the sun rose at Port Blair, about 330 km north of the clip’s coordinates, at 05:31 local time, and had set at 17:30 the evening before.22 Flying on from the last radar fix, the plane would have reached the clip’s position around local midnight, about five hours before dawn.

The advocates’ own weather check uses NASA’s night-time infrared passes.13

Why only suggestive. An infrared image can show clouds at night, so this does not settle it alone. It sits badly with clouds that match daytime photos taken over Japan.

9a

“Strange anomalies” and “briefing a foreign…”

Decisive⌄

Verdict: decisive that the released documents don’t show it. The released papers never mention MH370, satellites or video. The “brief” is a mine-warfare PowerPoint sent to a Taiwanese naval attaché in 2012–13, before MH370.

The case for

The post says his court documents hold “strange anomalies” and that he “openly admitted to briefing a foreign…”.11

The evidence against

Decisive Nothing released mentions MH370 (for “the court documents show it”; it says nothing about the classified addendum)

None of the 57 pages of the release (the appeals opinion, the court-martial order, the charge sheets, the plea agreement and the stipulation) mentions MH370, Malaysia, a missing aircraft, a satellite, a drone, imagery or a video.56

4Orbs agrees: “No released document explicitly names MH370 or satellite video.”12 Rolling Stone: “There is no publicly available evidence connecting the former serviceperson to the MH370 videos.”4

“Briefing a foreign…” almost certainly comes from Lin’s signed Stipulation of Fact. In it he says he met two Taiwanese naval attachés in Washington in 2012–13, and “I also forwarded CDR [redacted] a PowerPoint brief on mine warfare.” In October 2013 he went to Taiwan on a false leave form and met its navy chief.5 Both are before MH370, and neither mentions an aircraft, a satellite or a video.

Strong What he actually admitted disclosing

“Information relating to the capabilities of Special Projects Patrol Squadron TWO (VPU-2) and details of U.S. Pacific Fleet activities that were closely held”, plus details of a classified JTF 519 plan he had exercised.5

Who he told: the Taiwanese attachés, including the mine-warfare brief; a research fellow at the Formosa Association for Public Affairs who also worked for Taiwan’s Democratic Progressive Party, between April 2012 and May 2014; and an undercover FBI agent, over five meetings in Honolulu in August and September 2015.5

Of the agent he says she “never asked me to reveal national defense information… I knowingly tried to impress her.”5

None of these recipients is shown, or alleged in any record, to have posted anything online.

The evidence against

Open The investigation dates don’t point at a video either way (a coincidence the record explains without a video; it can’t be excluded from outside)

9e

Severity: the counts dropped, yet nine years

Strong⌄

Verdict: He pleaded to mishandling and communicating classified information. The twelve reviewed 13 hours of his interview talk about many programmes, the judge would not presume actual harm, and the government conceded it couldn’t corroborate much of the confession.

The case for

The espionage counts were dropped, yet he got nine years, the “right punishment” in Forbes’s words. Twelve classification authorities reviewed what he disclosed.412

The evidence against

Strong The sentence does not reveal the content

He pleaded guilty to three counts of breaking lawful general orders (carrying SECRET kneeboard cards through an airport, keeping SECRET notes at home, not reporting foreign contacts), two false official statements on leave forms, and two counts of communicating national defense information. The espionage counts were withdrawn under the plea deal.65

He was sentenced on 2 June 2017 to nine years’ confinement, loss of all pay and dismissal.5 The maximum was 36 years.6 Under the plea agreement, confinement beyond six years was suspended.5

The judge heard testimony on harm to national defense but said he would not presume any harm had actually occurred.6

The plea agreement lists stipulations from four of the authorities: PACOM, OPNAV, JSOC and AFRICOM. No page says what any of them reviewed.5

The twelve classification authorities reviewed 13 hours of his post-arrest interviews,6 in which he talked about many programmes: a long-range anti-ship missile, the rail gun, a laser weapon and torpedo tests.27 That explains the breadth without any video.

Strong Even the confession was thin (as context)

In November 2016 government lawyers “conceded that, despite Lin’s initial confession, they had no direct evidence corroborating much of what Lin supposedly confessed to”, with “little or no evidence he transferred classified information to Taiwanese officials aside from two emails that were classified ‘secret’ after the fact”.27

AP, June 2017: prosecutors “abandoned efforts to convict Lin of spying for China or Taiwan”.28

Verdict: Routine for counterintelligence files, and it shows nothing in either direction.

The case for

The SECRET addendum and the NCIS file are withheld, and the FBI won’t confirm or deny having records.12

The evidence against

Suggestive Withheld files are normal

Counterintelligence investigation files are routinely withheld as classified, and “neither confirm nor deny” is the FBI’s standard answer.12 Their absence shows nothing about what is in them, in either direction.

5What is known about the flight

As of 22 November 2026.

  • The flight. It left Kuala Lumpur at 00:42 Malaysia time on 8 March 2014 for Beijing: a Boeing 777-200ER with 227 passengers from 14 nations and 12 crew.1
  • Last contact. The last radio call was at 01:19:30: “Good night Malaysian three seven zero”. The plane left air-traffic radar at 01:21 near waypoint IGARI.1
  • The turn back. Military radar tracked it turning back across the Malay Peninsula, south of Penang and up the Strait of Malacca. The last radar contact was at 02:22, about 200 nautical miles west-north-west of Penang.1
  • The satellite data. Seven handshakes with Inmarsat’s satellite followed, the last at 08:19, after 7 hours 38 minutes in the air. Investigators read that as about when the fuel ran out. The handshakes are the only evidence of where it went after radar.1
  • Official findings. Malaysia declared the loss an accident on 29 January 2015, with all 239 aboard presumed dead.1 Malaysia’s 2018 safety report found the plane was diverted manually, “either by the pilot or a third party”, and could not say why.124 The ATSB’s final search report came out on 3 October 2017.20
  • Debris. A flaperon was confirmed in 2015. By 2018, 3 items were confirmed and 7 assessed almost certainly from MH370.1
  • The searches. 2014–17: Australia led a seabed search with Malaysia and China over more than 120,000 km², suspended on 17 January 2017. 2018: Ocean Infinity searched on a “no find, no fee” basis. 2025–26: a new Ocean Infinity search ended on 23 January 2026.1 Ocean Infinity said on 8 March 2026: “we can say with confidence that it isn’t where we looked.”7
  • Now. In June 2026 Malaysia extended the deal to 30 June 2027 to finish the remaining 7,428.54 km², with ships due back between November and April, weather allowing.29 We found no report, up to 22 November 2026, that they have returned.

Three takeaways

1 · The studio

The BioWare team itself grew only about 2.5×. Developers credited to the studio went from 182 on Mass Effect (2007) to 413 on Andromeda (2017) and 480 on Veilguard (2024). The fastest-growing studio disciplines were design (23 → 74 → 105, 4.6×), especially systems/combat design (3 → 22 → 33), and production/management (11 → 41 → 58, 5.3×). After those came in-game and technical animation (9 → 30 → 40). Studio engineering roughly doubled (57 → 131 → 111). Studio art roughly doubled too (45 → 79 → 98).

2 · Outside the studio

The big growth is outside the studio. Everyone credited went from 559 to 1,898 to 3,618 (3.4× to Andromeda, 6.5× to Veilguard). That growth came from four places:

  • EA central teams (186 → 824 → 1,803). The Frostbite engine organisation alone is 644 of Veilguard's credits.
  • Outside vendors. Named studios went from 7 to 20 to 41. People named from those vendors went from 27 to 26 to 790.
  • Localization (25 → 421 → 608).
  • QA (138 → 272 → 518).

Asset work in particular moved out of the building. On Veilguard, 274 outsourced artists are named, against 98 BioWare artists. EA's central animation and capture teams (83 people) and outside animation and mocap vendors (40) together outnumber BioWare's own 77 animators and cinematics staff.

3 · Your two questions

To Nick's two questions: no, it is not gameplay programming. Gameplay programmers can only be told apart from Inquisition onward, and they are flat (41 → 42 → 49). Studio engineering grew through online/multiplayer (0 → 30–38 on Inquisition and Andromeda, gone again on single-player Veilguard). It also grew through engine/tools work after the move to Frostbite (19 → 54), on top of hundreds of EA engine staff. Texture and model work grew, but mostly through outsourcing. In-house environment art went 14 → 39 and character art 8 → 22, both about 2.8×.

Caveats: credits are not headcount

  1. Vendors became people. Up to Andromeda, outsourcers were credited as company names only, for example "Additional Art: Liquid Development". Veilguard names 425 vendor staff individually, plus 233 temp-agency testers (Smartemp alone is 164). The 27 → 790 jump is partly a change in crediting. The rise from 7 to 41 named studios is real.
  2. EA credits whole central organisations. Veilguard credits all of EA Create's Frostbite (644), Creative Technologies (220), Data & Analytics (75) and SEED (46). These are shared, multi-game teams, not people assigned to Dragon Age. The publisher column is therefore an upper bound on publisher effort.
  3. Departed staff. Veilguard credits about 40 departed leads as "(Former)" and has a 111-person BioWare special-thanks block. Older credits mostly dropped people who left before ship, which flatters the growth of the core team slightly in Veilguard's favour. It also understates every team's real size.
  4. Foreign-language voice casts are credited inconsistently. DAO, ME3, Andromeda and Veilguard credit them; ME1 (Xbox), ME2 (partly) and Inquisition do not. The Localization row swings with this.
  5. Long, rebooted development. Veilguard was in development for about a decade with two reboots, so its credits accumulate people across years. ME1 was about 3.5 years. A credit count is people × "ever touched it", not peak team size, so budget scales with headcount × years.
  6. Classification is by title. BioWare's titles changed. ME1's "Technical Designers" did level scripting that later games credit as "Level Designers". Before Inquisition, programmers are listed only as "Programmers". Read the sub-discipline rows as directional.
  7. ME2's MobyGames credits look thin (532 people, no translation vendors listed). Treat the ME2 dip as partly a transcription gap.

Method

  • Source: the MobyGames credits transcriptions of each game's in-game credits. MobyGames sits behind a Cloudflare check (it returns 403 to curl, WebFetch and the headless browser), so I read Wayback Machine snapshots of the same pages. The same source was used for all seven games. Platforms: ME1 Xbox 360 (the original release; the 2008 PC port adds a porting team), Veilguard PS5 (MobyGames' credits for it), all others Windows.
  • Parsing: every credit row became (section, role, MobyGames person ID).
  • Deduplication: by MobyGames person ID within a game. MobyGames merges aliases, so "credited as" variants collapse. Each person gets one primary bucket: the first credit they hold in credit order. Two preferences apply. If they hold any BioWare-team credit, they count as BioWare. A development role beats a voice role, so a programmer who also did additional voices counts as a programmer. Buckets therefore add up to unique people. Open-source and middleware licence holders (e.g. 112 WebKit authors on Inquisition) are excluded. "Special thanks" is its own bucket.
  • Team: taken from the credit section.
    • Core means BioWare's own departments and studio operations.
    • Publisher means Microsoft Game Studios on ME1 and EA on the rest: corporate, Frostbite, central QA, localization, mocap, marketing, analytics and SEED.
    • External means vendors, temp-agency testers, contract composers and orchestras.
    • Cast means voice actors, with foreign-language casts counted under Localization.
  • Discipline: keyword rules on the role title, with the section as a fallback (classify.py). 1 of about 9,900 people is unclassified. Cinematic designers count under Animation & cinematics, not Design. QA programmers count under QA.
